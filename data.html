Data & methods

Data, methods, and documentation

The interactive viewer presents a reduced set of fields for visual exploration. This page provides access to the fuller metadata, the fixed layout data, the hotspot records, and the processed motif crops used in the published environment. It also summarises the method through which the corpus was constructed and interpreted.

Method in brief

The workflow connects corpus construction, motif extraction, metadata modelling, computational visualisation, and critical interpretation.

01

Corpus construction

Archaeological objects, publication images, and reception material were brought together as three related corpus layers.

02

Motif extraction

Individual motifs were isolated as cropped units and linked to their parent images.

03

Metadata modelling

Each crop was described through historical, iconographic, object, and source fields.

04

Computational visualisation

PixPlot generated image representations, a UMAP projection, and ten stored hotspots.

05

Critical interpretation

Visual neighbours were checked against metadata, parent images, publications, and provenance.

What the archive can and cannot show

Computed proximity supports comparison, but it does not carry the same evidentiary weight as provenance, publication history, and object documentation.

It can

  • Identify formal proximity within the prepared corpus and gather related forms across layers and media.
  • Place formally related motifs from different metadata categories in the same neighbourhood.
  • Turn a visual proximity into a question that can be checked against the record, parent image, and source of each crop.

It cannot

  • Decide whether one object directly influenced another; that depends on documentary and historical evidence.
  • Remove the effects of cropping, orientation, contrast, surface texture, and reproduction quality.
  • Keep every documented motif sequence together when its visual rendering changes across supports and media.

Provisional status. The archive currently joins its visualisation, metadata catalogue, downloadable data, and source links through GitHub Pages and the repository. A fuller implementation could introduce IIIF image services, linked metadata, more detailed rights statements, and evaluation with researchers or museum audiences.

Metadata and generated PixPlot data are publicly accessible. Source images are shared only where rights allow; other records link to the holding institution, publication, or digital platform.
