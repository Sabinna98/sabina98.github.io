What the archive can and cannot show

The visualisation is an instrument for exploration and comparison, not a substitute for art-historical and archaeological interpretation.

It can
  • Identify formal proximity within the prepared corpus and gather related forms across layers and media.
  • Place formally related motifs from different metadata categories in the same neighbourhood.
  • Turn a proximity into a question, checked against the record and the source of each crop.
It cannot
  • Decide whether one object directly influenced another; that rests on provenance and documentation.
  • Escape the image: position is also shaped by cropping, orientation, contrast, and reproduction.
  • Hold a documented motif together when its rendering changes across supports.
In its present state the archive is provisional: its three layers are joined through files and the PixPlot interface. A fuller realisation — with IIIF image standards, linked metadata, and one integrated interface — is a direction for further work.
