The Reception of Minoan Motifs in Modern and Contemporary Textiles and Fashion: Digital Analysis through Computational Image Clustering

A non-commercial academic research project developed as part of a Master’s thesis in Digital and Public Humanities at Ca’ Foscari University of Venice.

This project traces Minoan ornament as it moves from Bronze Age objects and their early publications into twentieth-century textiles, stage costume, and fashion. It brings dispersed material into one visual field, organised by visual similarity and tied to documentation and source, so that connections difficult to see across separate catalogues can be examined and tested.

What the archive holds

The corpus is organised through three historical layers: archaeological objects from Bronze Age Crete and the Aegean; the early publications through which their motifs first circulated; and the reception of those motifs in the textiles and garments of Mariano Fortuny, Léon Bakst, Florentini Kaloutsi, and Karl Lagerfeld’s Chloé Spring–Summer 1994 collection. Individual motifs were extracted as cropped units and described through a structured metadata scheme, so that each fragment stays tied to its object and its source.

The method

The crops are arranged by the visualisation tool PixPlot, which places images by computed visual similarity. The result is read against the metadata: the algorithm proposes a proximity, and the researcher checks it through source evidence, provenance, and close visual analysis. Computed nearness indicates a shared visual form, and it does not, on its own, establish a historical relation.

The visualisation is an instrument for exploration and comparison, not a substitute for art-historical and archaeological interpretation. Its groupings are proposals; their historical significance is evaluated through the records, parent images, publications, and external sources connected to the individual crops.
